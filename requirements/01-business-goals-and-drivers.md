# 01 · Business Goals & Drivers

## Who is the customer?

The 72nd Countess Von Digitalis, newly and unexpectedly in charge of a large, sprawling estate. The family's previous business (explosive garden gnomes) is gone. She needs the estate to pay for itself — and she needs to see that it does.

## Business goals

| # | Goal | Why it matters | Horizon |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | **Make the estate profitable** | The alternative is selling the carnivorous plant collection or returning to gnomes | Ongoing |
| G2 | **Grow attendance from ~5,000 to ≥15,000 visitors/day** | Explicit target in the brief | 3 years |
| G3 | **Increase returning visitors** | Repeat visits are cheaper to win than new ones and smooth seasonality | 12–18 months |
| G4 | **Know where to invest and where to put staff** | Today this is guesswork | 6 months |
| G5 | **Keep animals healthy and happy at lower cost** | Sick animals are expensive; healthy animals are the attraction | Ongoing |
| G6 | **Keep visitors safe** | Historic rides, poisonous animals, a family audience | Non-negotiable |

## Drivers behind the goals

- **Revenue:** ticket and family-pass sales, on-site spend, repeat visits.
- **Cost:** veterinary and feed costs, staffing, ride maintenance, cloud/AI spend.
- **Reputation:** an incident with a poisonous animal or an 18th-century ride would end the business; visitor trust in how their data is handled matters too.
- **Time:** the Countess has no legacy IT to protect and wants results within a season, not a decade.

## What "success" looks like from the Countess's chair

A daily view that answers: *How many people came, where did they go, what did they spend, are the animals well, and what should we change tomorrow?* — with AI doing the pattern-finding and humans making the decisions. That view is the [Estate daily report](../hld/core/README.md#estate-daily-report) (FR-2.7): one screen at 21:00, numbers verbatim, one recommendation for tomorrow.
